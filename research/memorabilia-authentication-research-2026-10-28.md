# Memorabilia authentication: research for Credabilia (2026-10-28)

Purpose: learn how the memorabilia world authenticates items, who the authenticators are, what went wrong in the past year, and what that means for Credabilia's credibility score and issuer list.

**How to read this.** Every claim carries a confidence tag.
- **[A]** two or more independent reputable sources, or the company's own primary page.
- **[B]** one reputable news outlet, or a company page that is partly marketing.
- **[C]** a dealer blog, forum, marketing, or a single secondary summary. Treat as a lead to verify, not a fact.
- **[?]** conflicting reports.

**Limits.** Research was done through web search summaries plus a few page fetches. Several key sites (Beckett's update page, Sports Collectors Daily, Heritage and Pristine results pages) blocked automated access, so some details come second-hand. No claim here should be shown to buyers without checking it against the primary source. Nothing here is legal advice.

## 2. Kinds of evidence (not all "authentication" is the same thing)

1. **Autograph authentication**: an authenticator compares the signature to exemplars. Output: hologram/sticker + certificate or letter, usually a lookup record. Examples: PSA/DNA, JSA, BAS.
2. **Witnessed signing**: the authenticator or manufacturer watches the person sign and tags it on the spot. Generally regarded as the strongest provenance [B]. Examples: Fanatics Authentic, Upper Deck Authenticated, Steiner Sports, PSA headquarters signings. Its limit: a hologram alone is not proof, and fakes copy holograms.
3. **Manufacturer-certified autographs**: pack-pulled on-card autos from Topps/Panini are certified by the manufacturer and do not usually need third-party authentication [C].
4. **Game-used / photomatch**: proves an item was used in a specific game by matching wear to photos or video. Examples: MeiGray, SIA, MEARS, Resolution Photomatching. Different question from "is the signature real".
5. **Card grading**: grades condition and checks for alteration. Examples: PSA, BGS, SGC, CGC, TAG.
6. **House guarantee or letter**: the auction house vouches (RR Auction lifetime guarantee [B], Heritage, Goldin, Hunt, Grey Flannel). Not independent of the seller's interest.
7. **Provenance / chain of custody**: documents, prior sales, estate letters. Hunt's $14M Jordan sale leaned on this.
8. **Studio / team documentation** for props and uniforms: a studio-issued certificate or signed letter from someone connected to the production is the strongest documentary evidence for screen-used items [C]. Screen-matching is the physical test.
9. **Technology**: serialized holograms (item and certificate must both match), digital records linked to photos, chips (MatchWornShirt), nanoparticle stickers (Metabilia), covert markings and blockchain claims (The Realest) [B]/[C]. None is independently validated in the sources found.

**Layering is normal.** JSA and MeiGray announced that from April 16, 2026 a single MeiGray submission can request both photomatching and JSA autograph authentication [B]. For the Ruth jersey, MeiGray, PSA and End-to-End matched it while Resolution Photomatching declined to match it in three reviews (2019, 2021, 2022) [B] ([ESPN](https://www.espn.com/mlb/story/_/id/40955990/babe-ruth-called-shot-jersey-auction-photo-matching-memorabilia), [cllct](https://www.cllct.com/sports-collectibles/auctions/heritage-adds-resolution-photomatching-letter-to-babe-ruth-jersey-auction)). Competent authenticators disagree.

## 4. Authenticator profiles

**In our system today:** PSA/DNA, JSA, BAS, SGC, CGC, Upper Deck Authenticated, Fanatics Authentic, Steiner, TriStar, MLB Authenticated, Fiterman, Frameworth, Other.

### Already listed: what changed or what to know
- **PSA/DNA** [B/C]: largest; keeps a large exemplar library; photographs every item and links hologram numbers to photos; reportedly stopped issuing physical cards for standard autograph authentication from about Oct 2023 [C]. A dealer blog says its liability guarantee is capped at $10,000 [C].
- **JSA** [B]: CCG-owned; strongest reputation for music, entertainment, vintage paper [C]; public verification at spenceloa.com. Looks for natural signature variation and live ink to catch autopens [B].
- **BAS (Beckett)** [B]: **systems offline since Sept 9, 2026** after "suspicious technical activity" during an IT upgrade; restoration in three phases, **BAS Certificate Lookup back Oct 15**, new autograph submissions in Phase 3 with no date [B/C, from Beckett's update page as summarized; page itself returned 403 to me]. Separate proposed class action (filed Aug 5, 2026) over a Nov 2025 data breach, about one million accounts [B]. **A BAS contractor, LeeAnn Branco, was charged in Pennsylvania (Jan 2025) over forged Jason Kelce signatures; the charges against her were later withdrawn.** Beckett sued the Rhode Island store and the contractor and said it would reimburse buyers [B] ([cllct](https://www.cllct.com/sports-collectibles/autographs/beckett-files-lawsuit-alleging-fraudulent-jason-kelce-autographs)). Lesson: an authenticator's individual contractors are a risk point.
- **SGC / CGC** [B]: card grading; CGC grows fastest. **CGC Signature Series** certifies only witnessed signatures; unwitnessed ones get a Qualified label or a CGC x JSA label [C].
- **Upper Deck Authenticated** [B]: hologram on item plus matching hologram on certificate; QR-code slab for Rookie Patch Autos [C, older].
- **Fanatics Authentic** [B]: witnessed signings, serialized hologram, FanSecure lookup. Changed its hologram after a counterfeiter copied the old one. A critique: no image of the genuine item is linked to the code [C].
- **Steiner Sports** [C]: hologram lookup since 2015; no 2026 change found.
- **TriStar** [B]: still active; says all autographs are authenticated by TriStar, PSA/DNA, JSA or other select companies.
- **MLB Authenticated** [B]: self-destroying stickers; the program does not cover items it did not witness.

## 6. Rules and marketplace policies

- **California** Civil Code 1739.7: a dealer selling autographed sports memorabilia to a consumer must give a written certificate of authenticity with an express warranty, describing the item and signer, price and sale date, retained for seven years; applies to items sold for $5 or more under the later amendment; a violation can bring up to 10x actual damages and may be an unfair business practice [B]. Whether an online marketplace counts as a "dealer" was raised in *Gentry v. eBay* but not decided [B]. **Needs a lawyer's read before Credabilia relies on or ignores it.**
- **New York** has a similar certificate rule (Arts and Cultural Affairs Law 60.02): signed, in at least ten-point boldface, with the dealer's legal name and address and an express warranty [B]. Connecticut had a certificate bill for items over $50 (1992); enactment unclear [C]. Florida, Texas, New Jersey and Illinois were not covered by the search.
- **eBay**: Authenticity Guarantee for trading cards at $200 and up (some sources say $250); accepted graders PSA, SGC, CGC, BGS; PSA is the official authenticator; signature confirmation for cards of $1,500 and up; raw redemption cards not eligible. eBay's autograph policy warns buyers unless they witnessed the signing; COAs may never be sold on their own [B].
- **Whatnot**: counterfeit policy with refunds, and sellers can submit evidence such as a COA; no required third-party authentication found [B].
- **Pristine Auction**: accepts only authenticators that pass its "integrity test" and provide a verifiable hologram or COA; may require more regardless; recognized list: JSA, PSA, Steiner, Mounted Memories, Schwartz, TriStar, Official Player Holograms, Upper Deck Authenticated, MLB Authentication; its own submissions go to BAS, JSA or PSA/DNA; items that fail are not sold [B].
- **Heritage**: autograph lots are not on approval; lots with a third-party certificate carry no right of return; a current "all autographs authenticated" rule was not confirmed [B/C].
- **Goldin**: says it does not authenticate in-house; submits ungraded or unauthenticated items to third parties; a failed item cancels the sale [C, FAQ may be old].

## 7. Category notes (we sell Sports, Comics, Art, Entertainment, History)

- **Sports cards**: manufacturer-certified autos; third-party grades; PSA dominates. Logoman and redemption items depend on the manufacturer's records; no source found on how patch authenticity is verified.
- **Comics**: CGC leads; CGC Signature Series for witnessed signatures; CBCS closed; PGX small; PSA entered July 2025.
- **Art**: authentication boards closed over litigation cost (Warhol, Calder, Lichtenstein, Basquiat, Haring); confidence now comes from catalogue raisonnés, scholars' opinions, lab science and auction vetting committees; opinions are framed as scholarship, not certification [B]. A "certificate" is rarely the evidence; the catalogue raisonné and provenance are.
- **Entertainment / music**: JSA is seen as strong for music; studio paperwork and screen-matching for props; stage-use needs its own documentation beyond the signature [C]. Disputes exist among music authenticators (e.g. Roger Epperson, allegations only) [C].
- **History / documents**: JSA, PSA/DNA and house letters (RR Auction guarantee); specific experts are contested; no independent audit found [C].
