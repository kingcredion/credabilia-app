The provenance of John Lennon’s recovered Framus Hootenanny 12-string

A guitar found in a British attic sold for $2,857,500 in May 2024. Between the discovery and the auction came a collector’s question: how could anyone tell this particular guitar was the one John Lennon played? Julien’s sale report

A guitar caught on film

Lennon can be seen playing the Framus in the film Help! during “You’ve Got to Hide Your Love Away.” Julien’s also identifies the instrument in photographs from the Beatles’ Help! and Rubber Soul sessions, where Lennon and George Harrison both played it. Julien’s account of the recordings

From musician to road manager

In his written authentication, Beatles historian Andy Babiuk traces the guitar from Lennon to Gordon Waller, of Peter and Gordon, by the end of 1965. Waller played it on a British television special and later gave it to the duo’s road manager. Babiuk’s ownership account

The attic find

More than half a century later, the guitar resurfaced in an attic. Julien’s account of the discovery also describes the recovery of its original case, another object the auction house could compare with period photographs. Julien’s discovery report

What the photographs showed

Babiuk examined Framus serial number 51083 in person. He compared the grain on the guitar’s body, the pattern of colored wood around its sound hole and the mottling of its imitation-tortoiseshell pickguard with archival photographs. His April 2024 report identifies the surviving instrument as the Beatles’ guitar. Read the authentication

The auction result

Julien’s reported the $2,857,500 sale at its Music Icons auction at the Hard Rock Cafe in Times Square. For collectors, the most instructive part of the record comes before the price: the report names the examiner and the individual features he matched, so readers can follow his reasoning.
