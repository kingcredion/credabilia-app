# Giveaway: state-by-state notes (researched 2026-10-14)

Not legal advice, and not lawyer-reviewed. Sources are the states' own statutes and agencies where I could reach them; law-firm and vendor summaries were used only to find which states to look at. Re-check before each giveaway. Design: docs/giveaway-and-tickets-spec.md.

## The short version
- Only **New York, Florida and Rhode Island** require registration and/or a bond for a promotional sweepstakes, and only above a prize threshold (NY and FL: total prizes over $5,000; RI: retail-store promotions over $500). Every other state relies on general lottery law (prize + chance + payment = illegal lottery) plus consumer-protection law.
- Our giveaway has no payment of any kind (free entry two ways, nothing earned by buying), so it is not a lottery. The prize is under $5,000 (enforced in the database), and NY, FL and RI are excluded anyway.
- A few states have **disclosure** laws for prize offers (Maryland, Washington, Illinois, Wisconsin). They are aimed at "you may have won" notices and mailings, but the disclosures are cheap to include, so the rules and card carry them everywhere.
- Three states have an age of majority above 18: Alabama and Nebraska 19, Mississippi 21. The entry form and rules handle this.

## What each finding means for us
| State / topic | What the source says | What we do |
|---|---|---|
| New York (GBL 369-e) | Register (30 days before per vendor summaries; DOS form says registration with $100 fee) and post a bond for the full prize value when total prizes exceed $5,000; winner certification within 90 days | Prize must be under $5,000 (admin refuses); NY residents excluded |
| Florida (849.094, FAC 5J-14) | File 7 days before, $100 fee, trust account or bond, winner list within 60 days when prizes exceed $5,000; applies to promotions open to Florida residents | Same: under $5,000; FL excluded |
| Rhode Island (RIGL 11-50-1 to -5) | Registration ($150, Form 660) for retail-establishment promotions with prizes over $500; online-only reportedly outside it, but the statute text says retail establishment | RI excluded (conservative) |
| Maryland (Com. Law 13-305(g)) | Chance promotions: disclose exact number of prizes, retail value, odds (or that they depend on the number of entries), whether all prizes will be awarded and when winners are determined, conditions to receive the prize, where and when the winner list (for prizes over $100) can be obtained, and no purchase necessary | All of these are now in the rules (our prize is about $125, so the winner-list item applies: 90 days by email) |
| Washington (RCW 19.170) | Offers to Washington residents must name promoter/sponsor and address, state verifiable retail value of each prize, and odds; no calling something a "prize" if you must spend money to get it | Sponsor name/address, prize value and odds statement are in the rules and on the card; nothing to pay |
| Illinois (815 ILCS 525) | Written offers: sponsor name/address, retail value, no purchase necessary, purchase will not improve odds, odds, any charges, restrictions/eligibility; no payment as a condition of a prize | In the rules; no charges |
| Wisconsin (100.171) | Prize notices: no payment before a written notice with disclosures; no false "you won" claims | We only tell real winners; no payment ever |
| Arizona (Dept of Gaming guidance) | A free route must match a paid route in manner, extent and odds, be shown conspicuously, and carry no extra hurdles | There is no paid route at all; the free email route is shown on the card and in the entry form |
| Tennessee (39-17-506) | Giveaways are lawful only if people can enter without paying or buying | No payment anywhere |
| Nevada (NRS 462, our home state) | Defines lottery; I could not read the full definition or find a Nevada rule for no-purchase promotions. "Consideration" is paying value | No consideration. Worth a free email to the Nevada Gaming Control Board or the Attorney General's consumer office to confirm in writing |
| Texas, Michigan | General lottery law (prize, chance, consideration) and consumer protection; bills about prize-notice acts were found but not confirmed as law | Nothing extra |
| Age of majority | AL 19 (Ala. Code 26-1-1), NE 19 (Neb. Rev. Stat. 43-2101), MS 21 (Miss. Code 1-3-27; 18 for property contracts) | Entry checkbox shows the state's age; rules say so |
| "Sweepstakes casinos" | Many states (CT, MI, MD, WV, LA, NJ, TN and others) are banning dual-currency casino-style sweepstakes | Not what this is: free entry, one prize, no currency |

## Rules of the road for every giveaway
1. Prize under $5,000, one prize, fixed end date, no early end.
2. Free entry: never charge, never give tickets for buying or selling, never imply a purchase helps.
3. Keep the rules page, card and item page saying the same thing (value, dates, states, how to enter).
4. Tell winners they won only after the draw. Ship within 30 days of the address.
5. Keep entry records and the winner list for at least 90 days (Florida: winning entries 90 days; Rhode Island: records six months, so keep six months).
6. Social media: do not require sharing, tagging or posting to enter (Facebook and Instagram promotion rules), and anyone promoting it for a reward must say so (FTC endorsement rules). The referral link is not a social-post requirement.
